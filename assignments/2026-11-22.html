Empathy: Imagining How Someone Else Feels

Read the lesson together, take the 5-question quiz, then play the feelings detective game.

💛 Feelings · Ages 7–10 · About 10 min

Stepping Into Someone Else's Shoes

Have you ever seen a friend cry and felt a little sad too? That caring feeling is part of empathy. Empathy means trying to understand how someone else feels by imagining what it would be like to be in their place.

People don't always say their feelings out loud, so we can be feeling detectives and look for clues:

  • Faces: a big smile, a frown, wide eyes, or a shaking lip.
  • Bodies: jumping up and down, slumped shoulders, crossed arms, or hiding behind a parent.
  • Voices: loud and excited, quiet and slow, or shaky.
  • What happened: losing a toy, winning a race, or moving to a new school.

Then use the three empathy steps:

  • 1. Notice: "Jordan is sitting alone and looking at the ground."
  • 2. Imagine: "If that were me, I might feel lonely."
  • 3. Care: Ask, "Are you okay? Do you want to play?" and then really listen to the answer.

Sometimes our guess is wrong, and that's okay! Jordan might just be tired. That's why asking and listening matter so much. And remember: other people can feel differently than you do, even in the same moment, and all feelings are real.

This lesson is written in our own words, based on common, kid-friendly ideas about empathy and social-emotional learning.

🕵️ Try it: The feelings detective game

  1. Play with a grown-up, brother, sister, or friend. Take turns being the actor and the detective.
  2. The actor secretly picks a feeling: happy, sad, surprised, worried, proud, or frustrated.
  3. Without using words, the actor shows the feeling with their face and body for 10 seconds.
  4. The detective guesses the feeling and says which clues helped, like "Your eyebrows went up, so I guessed surprised."
  5. Then the detective says one kind thing they could say or do for someone with that feeling.

Think about it: Remember a time someone showed you empathy when you felt sad, scared, or left out. What did they do, and how did it make you feel?

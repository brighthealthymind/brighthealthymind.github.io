Hibernation: How Animals Sleep Through Winter

Read the lesson together, take the 5-question quiz, then try a cozy-den experiment at home.

🐻 Science · Ages 7–10 · About 10 min

The Big Winter Rest

When winter comes, the days get cold, plants stop growing, and many bugs disappear. That means food gets hard to find. Some animals solve this problem in a clever way: they hibernate.

Hibernation is a very deep, long rest. It is much deeper than your sleep at night. While an animal hibernates, its body slows way down:

  • Its heart beats much more slowly. A groundhog's heart can slow down to just a few beats a minute.
  • It breathes only now and then.
  • Its body gets cooler. Some small hibernators get almost as cold as the air around them.

All this slowing down saves energy. But where does the energy come from? In the fall, hibernating animals eat and eat to build up body fat. All winter, their bodies slowly use that stored fat, like a packed lunch that has to last for months.

Who hibernates? Groundhogs, many kinds of bats, and some chipmunks are good examples. Bears rest in dens all winter too. Their bodies don't get as cold, but they can go for months without eating or drinking.

Other winter plans: Not every animal hibernates. Many birds migrate, flying to warmer places. Gray squirrels stay busy and dig up the nuts they hid in the fall. Every animal has its own way to make it to spring!

This lesson is written in our own words, based on basic life science facts shared by sources like the U.S. National Park Service and the National Wildlife Federation.

🧦 Try it: Build a cozy den

  1. Ask a grown-up to fill two cups with the same amount of warm (not hot) tap water.
  2. Wrap one cup snugly in a thick sock, a towel, or a small blanket. This is your cozy "den." Leave the other cup uncovered.
  3. Wait about 20 minutes.
  4. Unwrap the den cup and touch the water in both cups with a finger. Which one stayed warmer?

Think about it: Why do you think hibernating animals choose snug places like underground burrows, caves, or hollow logs to rest in? How is that like your cozy-den cup?

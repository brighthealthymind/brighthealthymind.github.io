Measuring Length: Inches and Centimeters

Read the lesson together, take the 5-question quiz, then go on a measuring hunt around your home with a ruler.

📏 Math · Ages 7–10 · About 10 min

How Long Is It?

How long is your pencil? How wide is your hand? To answer questions like these, we measure length. Length tells us how long something is from one end to the other. We use a tool called a ruler, and we count in units that are always the same size, so everyone gets the same answer.

Two units on one ruler. Many rulers have two sides. One side shows centimeters (cm), and the other shows inches (in). A centimeter is small, about as wide as your pinky fingernail. An inch is bigger: one inch is about 2 and a half centimeters. Because centimeters are smaller, it takes more of them to measure the same thing. A pencil might be about 7 inches long, but about 18 centimeters long. It's the same pencil, just counted with different-sized steps!

Start at zero. Here's the most important rule: line up one end of the object with the 0 mark on the ruler, not the very edge of the ruler and not the 1. Then look at where the other end of the object stops. That number is its length. If you start at 1 by mistake, your answer will be 1 unit too long.

Bigger units. For longer things, we use bigger units. 12 inches make 1 foot, and 100 centimeters make 1 meter. A door is about 2 meters tall, so you wouldn't want to measure it in tiny centimeters one at a time!

Facts checked against NIST's guide to units of length and Khan Academy's lessons on measuring length, explained in our own words.

📏 Try it: The measuring hunt

  1. Find a ruler that shows inches and centimeters.
  2. Pick 4 small things at home, like a spoon, a crayon, a sock, and a book.
  3. Before measuring each one, guess how many centimeters long it is.
  4. Line one end up with the 0 mark, and read the number where the other end stops. Write it down.
  5. Now flip the ruler and measure the same things in inches. Which number is bigger for each object, inches or centimeters?

Think about it: Why do you think the number of centimeters is always bigger than the number of inches for the same object?

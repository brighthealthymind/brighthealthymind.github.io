How Are Rainbows Made?

Read the lesson together, take the 5-question quiz, then make a rainbow of your own.

🌈 Science · Ages 7–10 · About 10 min

Sunlight + Raindrops = Rainbow

Have you ever looked up after a rain shower and spotted a giant stripe of colors across the sky? A rainbow isn't a solid thing you could touch. It's a light show made by two everyday things working together: sunlight and drops of water.

First, a secret about sunlight. It looks plain white, but it is really a mix of many colors all traveling together: red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo and violet. Normally they're blended so well that we can't tell them apart.

When sunlight goes into a raindrop, it slows down and bends, because light moves more slowly through water than through air. Scientists call this bending refraction (say it: ree-FRAK-shun). Then the light bounces off the back of the drop, like a ball off a wall, and bends again as it comes back out.

Here's the trick that makes the colors: each color bends a slightly different amount. Violet bends the most and red bends the least. So the colors that went in together come out spread apart, and millions of raindrops send them to your eyes as stripes. Red ends up on the top of the bow and violet on the bottom.

To see a rainbow, you need the Sun behind you and rain or mist in front of you. Rainbows show up most often in the early morning or late afternoon, when the Sun is low in the sky. And a rainbow is really shaped like a circle! The ground usually hides the bottom part, which is why we see a bow.

Lucky days bring a double rainbow. The second bow is fainter and sits above the first one, and its colors are flipped, with red on the bottom. It happens when light bounces twice inside the raindrops.

Facts checked with NOAA: What Causes a Rainbow?, the National Weather Service: How Do Rainbows Form?, and UCAR Center for Science Education: How Rainbows Are Made.

🌦️ Hands-on activity · About 15 min

Make your own rainbow

You need: a sunny day and a grown-up. Pick one way to try it. Never look straight at the Sun.

  1. Garden hose way (outside): Stand with the Sun behind you. Ask a grown-up to set the hose to a fine mist and spray it into the air in front of you. Look into the mist for your rainbow. Morning or late afternoon works best.
  2. Glass of water way (inside): Fill a clear glass with water and set it on a sunny windowsill. Put a sheet of white paper on the floor where the light lands, and move the paper or the glass a little until you see a small band of colors.
  3. Name the colors you can see, in order. Can you spot all seven, or do some blend together?
  4. Try turning around so you face the Sun side instead. What happens to your rainbow?

Think about it: Why can't you walk up to a rainbow and touch it? Use what you learned about where the light comes from, and talk about your idea with a grown-up.

Your Amazing Skeleton: Why Do We Have Bones?

Read the lesson together, take the 5-question quiz, then go on a joint hunt to find all the places your body bends.

🦴 Science · Ages 7–10 · About 10 min

The Frame Inside You

Without bones, you would flop like a wiggly noodle! Inside your body is a skeleton, a frame made of bones. A grown-up's skeleton has 206 bones. Babies are born with around 300 bone parts, but as they grow, some of those parts join together into bigger bones.

Job 1: Holding you up. Your skeleton gives your body its shape and helps you stand tall, the way the poles of a tent hold the tent up.

Job 2: Protecting you. Some bones work like a helmet or a shield. Your skull protects your brain, and your ribs make a cage that protects your heart and lungs.

Job 3: Helping you move. Muscles are attached to bones. When a muscle pulls on a bone, that part of your body moves. The places where two bones meet are called joints. Your knees, elbows, shoulders, and knuckles are all joints, and they let you bend, twist, and wave.

Bones are alive! Bones may look hard like rock, but they are living parts of you. They grow as you grow, and a broken bone can slowly heal itself. Inside many bones is soft marrow that makes new blood cells. The longest bone is the thigh bone (femur) in your upper leg, and the tiniest bone is deep inside your ear.

Keeping bones strong. Bones need calcium, found in foods like milk, yogurt, cheese, and leafy greens. Running, jumping, and playing also help bones grow strong.

Facts checked against Nemours KidsHealth's guide to bones and the U.S. National Library of Medicine (MedlinePlus), explained in our own words.

🦴 Try it: The joint hunt

  1. Stand up and slowly bend each part of your body that can bend: fingers, wrists, elbows, shoulders, neck, hips, knees, ankles, and toes.
  2. Each place that bends is a joint. Count how many different joints you can find on one side of your body.
  3. Notice how they move. Does your elbow bend only one way, like a door hinge? Can your shoulder swing in a big circle?
  4. Gently tap your knee, your forehead, and your ribs. Can you feel the hard bone under your skin?

Think about it: Imagine your arm had no elbow joint and was one long straight bone. What everyday things would be hard to do?

Sources: KidsHealth: Your Bones · MedlinePlus: Bones, Joints and Muscles

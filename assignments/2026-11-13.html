Area: Counting the Squares Inside a Shape

Read the lesson together, take the 5-question quiz, then cover a shape at home with sticky notes or paper squares.

🟩 Math · Ages 7–10 · About 10 min

How Much Space Does It Cover?

Earlier this week we learned about perimeter, the distance all the way around a shape. Today we're learning its partner, area. Area tells us how much flat space is inside a shape. It's like asking, "How many tiles would I need to cover this floor?"

Square units. We measure area with little squares that are all the same size, called square units. If each side of a square is 1 centimeter long, that square is 1 square centimeter. To find the area, we count how many squares fit inside the shape with no gaps and no overlaps.

A rows-and-columns shortcut. Picture a chocolate bar with 3 rows of 4 squares. You could count every square: 1, 2, 3... all the way to 12. Or you could notice there are 3 equal rows of 4, and multiply: 3 × 4 = 12. For any rectangle, area = length × width. That's the same as rows times columns!

Area or perimeter? Here's a good way to tell them apart. A fence goes around a garden, so it's perimeter. A carpet covers the inside of a floor, so it's area. Perimeter is measured in plain units like centimeters, and area is measured in square units like square centimeters.

Facts checked against Britannica's entry on area and Khan Academy's lessons on area for grade 3, explained in our own words.

📐 Try it: Cover it with squares

  1. Grab some sticky notes, or cut a sheet of paper into squares that are all the same size.
  2. Pick a flat rectangle at home, like a book cover, a placemat, or a cutting board.
  3. Before you start, guess how many squares it will take to cover it.
  4. Lay the squares in neat rows with no gaps and no overlaps. Count them all.
  5. Now count the squares in one row and the number of rows, and multiply. Do you get the same answer?

Think about it: If you used bigger squares, would you need more squares or fewer to cover the same shape? Why?

Your Hard-Working Heart: How Blood Gets Around

Read the lesson together, take the 5-question quiz, then find and count your own heartbeat.

❤️ Science · Ages 7–10 · About 10 min

The Pump Inside Your Chest

Put your hand flat on the middle of your chest, a little to the left. Can you feel a soft thump-thump? That is your heart, and it has been working every second of your life, even while you sleep!

A super muscle. Your heart is a strong muscle about the size of your fist. Its job is to pump blood all around your body. Each time it squeezes, it pushes blood out. Each time it relaxes, it fills up again. One squeeze and relax is one heartbeat.

Why does blood need to travel? Every part of you, from your brain to your toes, needs oxygen from the air you breathe. Blood is the delivery truck that carries it.

  • Stop 1, the lungs: The right side of the heart sends blood to your lungs, where it picks up fresh oxygen.
  • Stop 2, the whole body: The oxygen-rich blood comes back to the left side of the heart, which pumps it out to every part of your body.
  • Back home: After it drops off the oxygen, the blood returns to the heart, and the trip starts all over again.

Blood's roads. Blood travels through tubes called blood vessels. Arteries carry blood away from the heart. Veins carry blood back to the heart. A trick to remember: Arteries go Away.

Lub-dub! Inside your heart are little doors called valves. They open to let blood through and close so it can't flow backward. When a doctor listens with a stethoscope, the "lub-dub" sound is the valves closing.

Faster when you move. When you run or jump, your muscles need more oxygen, so your heart beats faster to deliver it. When you rest, it slows down again. Playing actively helps keep your heart strong.

Facts checked against KidsHealth (Nemours) and the American Heart Association, explained in our own words.

❤️ Try it: Count your heartbeat

  1. Sit quietly for one minute. Then gently press two fingers (not your thumb) on the inside of your wrist, below your thumb, until you feel a little beat. That is your pulse.
  2. With a grown-up timing, count the beats for 15 seconds. Multiply by 4 (or add the number to itself four times) to get beats per minute. Write it down.
  3. Now jump in place or do jumping jacks for 30 seconds.
  4. Count your pulse for 15 seconds again and multiply by 4. Is the number bigger or smaller than before?

Think about it: Why do you think your heart speeds up when you move, and slows down when you rest? What is your body asking for?

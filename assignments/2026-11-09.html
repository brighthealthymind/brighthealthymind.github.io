Perimeter: Measuring Around a Shape

Read the lesson together, take the 5-question quiz, then go on a perimeter hunt around your home.

📏 Math · Ages 7–10 · About 10 min

Walking All the Way Around

Imagine an ant walking along the edge of a sandwich, starting at one corner and going all the way around until it gets back to where it started. The total distance the ant walked is called the perimeter. Perimeter is the distance around the outside of a flat shape.

How to find it. To find the perimeter, measure every side and add them all together. A triangle with sides of 3 cm, 4 cm, and 5 cm has a perimeter of 3 + 4 + 5 = 12 cm. The trick is to count every side once, without skipping any and without counting any twice.

Shortcuts for rectangles and squares. A rectangle has two long sides that match and two short sides that match. A rectangle that is 6 cm long and 2 cm wide has sides of 6, 2, 6, and 2, so its perimeter is 6 + 2 + 6 + 2 = 16 cm. A square has four equal sides, so if one side is 5 cm, the perimeter is 5 + 5 + 5 + 5 = 20 cm, which is the same as 4 × 5.

Units matter. Perimeter is a length, so we use length units like centimeters, inches, meters, or feet. People use perimeter in real life all the time, like figuring out how much fence goes around a garden or how much ribbon goes around the edge of a picture frame.

Facts checked against Britannica's entry on perimeter and Math Is Fun's perimeter guide, explained in our own words.

🏠 Try it: Go on a perimeter hunt

  1. Find three flat, rectangle-shaped things at home, like a book, a placemat, or a cereal box front.
  2. Guess which one has the biggest perimeter and write your guess down.
  3. Use a ruler or measuring tape to measure each side. Round to the nearest whole centimeter or inch.
  4. Add the sides together to find each perimeter. Then check: was your guess right?

Think about it: Can two shapes that look very different have the same perimeter? Try making a long, skinny rectangle and a square out of the same piece of string to find out.

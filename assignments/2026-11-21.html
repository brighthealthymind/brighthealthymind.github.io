Adding with Regrouping: Carrying the Ten

Read the lesson together, take the 5-question quiz, then build an addition problem with bundles of ten.

➕ Math · Ages 7–10 · About 10 min

When the Ones Get Too Full

Every two-digit number has two places. The ones place is on the right, and the tens place is on the left. In 46, the 6 means 6 ones and the 4 means 4 tens, or 40.

When we add two numbers, we line them up and add the ones first. But what happens when the ones add up to 10 or more? A place can only hold one digit! So we regroup: we trade 10 ones for 1 ten. Some people call this "carrying."

Let's try 46 + 38 together.

  • Step 1, add the ones: 6 + 8 = 14. That's 1 ten and 4 ones. Write the 4 in the ones place.
  • Step 2, regroup: Carry the 1 ten over to the top of the tens column, like moving a bundle of ten sticks to the tens pile.
  • Step 3, add the tens: 4 + 3 + the 1 we carried = 8 tens. Write 8 in the tens place.
  • Answer: 46 + 38 = 84.

Check with a quick estimate. 46 is close to 50 and 38 is close to 40, and 50 + 40 = 90. Our answer, 84, is close to 90, so it makes sense. If you ever get a giant answer like 714, that's a clue you forgot to regroup!

Every sum in this lesson and quiz was double-checked, and it is all explained in our own words.

🥢 Try it: Add with bundles of ten

  1. Find about 50 small things you can group, like dry beans, buttons, or paper strips, and a few small cups.
  2. Show 27: put 2 cups of 10 on the left (tens) and 7 loose pieces on the right (ones).
  3. Now show 15 below it: 1 cup of 10 and 5 loose pieces.
  4. Push all the loose ones together and count them: 7 + 5 = 12. You have enough to fill a new cup of 10! Fill it and move it to the tens side. 2 loose ones are left.
  5. Count the cups (4 tens) and the loose ones (2). You just showed that 27 + 15 = 42.

Think about it: Make up your own addition problem where the ones add up to 10 or more. Can you explain to a grown-up why you needed to make a new group of ten?

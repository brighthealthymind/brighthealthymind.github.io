How Do Plants Make Their Own Food?

Read the lesson together, take the 5-question quiz, then do a sunny-window plant watch at home.

🌱 Science · Ages 7–10 · About 10 min

Plants Are Little Food Factories

When you're hungry, you grab a snack. But a plant can't walk to the kitchen! Instead, green plants make their own food right inside their leaves. This amazing job is called photosynthesis (say it: foh-toh-SIN-thuh-sis). "Photo" means light, and "synthesis" means putting things together.

Three ingredients. To make food, a plant needs three things. It needs sunlight, which gives it energy. It needs water, which its roots soak up from the soil and send up the stem. And it needs carbon dioxide, a gas in the air that slips into the leaves through tiny holes, much too small to see, called stomata.

Why leaves are green. Leaves are full of a green material called chlorophyll (say it: KLOR-uh-fill). Chlorophyll is like a solar panel. It catches the energy in sunlight so the leaf can use it to turn water and carbon dioxide into a kind of sugar. The plant uses that sugar for energy and to build new leaves, stems, roots, flowers, and fruit.

A gift for us. While plants make their sugar, they give off oxygen into the air. That's the gas people and animals need to breathe! And when we eat fruits, vegetables, grains, or nuts, we're eating energy that a plant first caught from the Sun.

Facts checked against National Geographic Education's resource on photosynthesis and Britannica's entry on photosynthesis, explained in our own words.

🪴 Try it: The sunny-window plant watch

  1. Find a houseplant that sits near a window, or ask a grown-up to help you put one there.
  2. Look closely at its leaves. Which way are most of them facing? Draw a quick picture.
  3. With a grown-up's OK, turn the pot halfway around so the leaves face away from the window.
  4. Check the plant every day for a week and draw it again. What do you notice?

Think about it: Many plants slowly bend their leaves and stems toward the light. Using what you learned today, why would a plant want to face the Sun?

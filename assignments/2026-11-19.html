Skip Counting: Hopping by 2s, 5s, and 10s

Read the lesson together, take the 5-question quiz, then count your family's fingers by 5s.

🐸 Math · Ages 7–10 · About 10 min

Counting With Big Hops

Imagine a frog on a number line. A tiny frog hops one number at a time: 1, 2, 3, 4… That works, but it is slow! A big frog can skip over numbers and land on every 2nd, 5th, or 10th one. Counting this way is called skip counting.

Hopping by 2s. Start at 2 and add 2 each time: 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12… These are the even numbers. Skip counting by 2s is great for counting things that come in pairs, like socks, shoes, or eyes.

Hopping by 5s. Start at 5 and add 5 each time: 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30… Look at the last digit: it always goes 5, 0, 5, 0. Counting by 5s helps with fingers on hands, nickels, and the minutes on a clock.

Hopping by 10s. Start at 10 and add 10 each time: 10, 20, 30, 40, 50… The last digit is always 0, and the tens digit goes up by one each hop. Counting by 10s helps with dimes and with toes on a group of people.

  • Why it helps: Counting 6 pairs of shoes one by one takes 12 counts. Counting by 2s takes only 6 hops: 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12.
  • Check your hops: Every hop is the same size. If you count by 5s and land on a number that doesn't end in 5 or 0, one hop went wrong.
  • A sneak peek: Skip counting is the secret helper behind multiplication. 4 hops of 5 lands on 20, and that's the same as 4 × 5 = 20.

Every number in this lesson was double-checked by counting, and it is explained in our own words.

✋ Try it: Count fingers by 5s

  1. Gather your family or a few stuffed animals with "hands" drawn on paper.
  2. Have everyone hold up both hands. Each hand has 5 fingers.
  3. Point to one hand at a time and count by 5s: 5, 10, 15, 20…
  4. Now count the same fingers one by one. Did you get the same number? Which way was faster?

Think about it: Can you find something at home that comes in groups of 2, 5, or 10? How would skip counting help you count it?

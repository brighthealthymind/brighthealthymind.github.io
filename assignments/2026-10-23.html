Your Body's Feelings Alarm: Calming Big Feelings

Read the lesson together, take the 5-question quiz, then practice a calm-down breathing trick.

💛 Feelings · Ages 7–10 · About 10 min

When Feelings Get Big, Your Body Notices

Have you ever felt so angry or scared that your heart started pounding? That's not just in your head. Your brain and body work together, and big feelings show up in your body too.

Your brain has a built-in alarm system. When it thinks something might be a problem, like a loud bang, a scary dog, or someone grabbing your toy, it gets your body ready to act fast. Your heart beats faster, your breathing gets quick, your muscles get tight, and you might feel hot, shaky, or have "butterflies" in your tummy.

This alarm is actually helpful. Long ago it helped people run from danger, and today it can help you jump out of the way of a ball. But sometimes the alarm goes off when there's no real danger, like when you lose a game or feel nervous before a test. When the alarm is loud, it's hard to think clearly and make good choices.

Here's the good news: you can help turn the alarm down. One of the best tools is slow belly breathing. When you breathe in slowly through your nose, let your belly fill up like a balloon, and breathe out even more slowly, it sends a message to your body that you are safe. Little by little, your heart slows down and your muscles relax.

Noticing your body's clues is a superpower. If you catch the early signs, like a hot face or clenched fists, you can stop, breathe, and name the feeling ("I feel frustrated") before you do something you might feel sorry about later.

Remember, all feelings are okay, even anger and fear. Calming down doesn't mean hiding your feelings. It means getting calm enough to figure out what to do about them, like talking to a grown-up you trust.

🎈 Hands-on activity · About 10 min

Balloon belly breathing and a body clue check

You need: a quiet spot, a small stuffed animal or a soft toy, and a piece of paper and a crayon.

  1. Lie down on your back and put the stuffed animal on your belly.
  2. Breathe in slowly through your nose while you count to 4 in your head. Watch the toy rise as your belly fills up like a balloon.
  3. Breathe out slowly through your mouth while you count to 6, like you're gently blowing out a birthday candle. Watch the toy go down.
  4. Do this 5 times. Then notice: does your body feel any different than before?
  5. On your paper, draw an outline of a body. Color the places where you feel clues when you're angry or scared, like your face, chest, hands, or tummy.

Think about it: What is one clue your body gives you when a big feeling is starting? Next time you notice that clue, what calm-down trick could you try?

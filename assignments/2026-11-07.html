Greater Than, Less Than, Equal: Comparing Numbers

Read the lesson together, take the 5-question quiz, then go on a quick number hunt around your home.

🔢 Math · Ages 7–10 · About 10 min

Which Number Wins?

Sometimes you need to know which number is bigger, which is smaller, or whether two numbers are the same. Math gives us three simple symbols to say that quickly.

Greater than (>). This means the first number is bigger. Example: 9 > 4 (nine is greater than four). A helpful trick: the open side of the symbol faces the bigger number, like a hungry mouth that wants the larger snack.

Less than (<). This means the first number is smaller. Example: 3 < 8 (three is less than eight). The pointed tip points at the smaller number.

Equal (=). This means both sides are the same. Example: 6 = 6. Balance scales that sit flat are equal.

How to compare two numbers. Start on the left with the highest place value (hundreds, then tens, then ones). The number with a bigger digit in the first place that is different is the greater number. If every digit matches, the numbers are equal.

Ideas aligned with everyday place-value comparing taught in early elementary math (Common Core Operations & Algebraic Thinking / Number & Operations in Base Ten).

🏠 Try it: Number hunt

  1. Find three numbers around your home (a clock, a book page, a food package, or house numbers).
  2. Write each number down.
  3. Put them in order from least to greatest.
  4. Write two true comparison sentences using >, <, or =.

Think about it: If two numbers have the same tens digit, what do you look at next to decide which is greater?
